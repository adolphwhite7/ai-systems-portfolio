# Enterprise Contact-Center Integration

**Role:** Integration architecture and technical leadership, Capital One<br>
**Period:** Capital One employment, October 1999–July 2020<br>
**Evidence:** [My published career account](https://mastermindgroupventures.com/founder.html)

## The Engineering Problem

Contact-center service depends on several systems working together: telephony, networks, security controls, desktops, cloud services, and the teams that operate them. A technically correct change in one area can disrupt another. My work focused on making those dependencies visible and coordinating a design the teams could implement and support.

## HSBC Acquisition: Six Acquired Sites

As lead integration architect for the contact-center work following Capital One's acquisition of HSBC's U.S. credit-card portfolio, I coordinated network replacement, firewall changes, desktop migration, and hardware upgrades across six acquired sites.

Technology and operations teams worked from a shared Master Execution Plan so sequencing and dependencies were explicit. Reorganizing network addresses and using network address translation reduced the required firewall rules from roughly 4,000 to 30, simplifying the environment the teams had to manage.

This example covers the six acquired contact centers. It is not a count of every site in my broader career responsibilities.

## Amazon Connect: Telephony and Cloud Access

As telephony integration lead for Capital One's Amazon Connect rollout, I worked with cloud engineering and information security on controlled access to cloud-stored data. Because traditional firewall separation was unavailable, the design used storage policies to address the access boundary and Payment Card Industry requirements.

My published account describes the PCI office adopting that policy approach as a reference for later work. I also created end-to-end diagrams connecting phone systems, call centers, networks, firewalls, and cloud services so delivery and operating teams had a shared view.

## What This Demonstrates

- Integration architecture across infrastructure, applications, security, and operations.
- Translating system dependencies into a coordinated execution plan.
- Simplifying network and access-control designs while working with the teams responsible for security and support.
